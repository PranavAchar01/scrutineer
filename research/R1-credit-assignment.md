# R1 — Component-level credit assignment in compound / multi-agent LLM systems (2025–2026)

Researched 2026-09-03 on the live web only. Every claim carries a URL and the date the source was posted/revised. "Hackathon-feasible" means buildable inside the 26-hour window against W&B Weave traces by a small team. Items marked UNVERIFIED were not confirmable from a fetched page.

---

## Summary

1. **Nobody has solved instance-level blame.** The best published attributors identify the *responsible agent* in a failed trace 54–70% of the time and the *decisive step* only 14–33% of the time (Who&When, ICML 2025; AgenTracer, ICLR 2026; TraceElephant, ACL 2026). Blame is far more reliable **statistically across many runs** than for any single run — Who&When states this explicitly. Design consequence: accumulate per-role blame over many races ("standings"), never upgrade a role on the evidence of one lap.
2. **The 2026 literature converged on counterfactual/ablation credit as the ground truth.** SHARP (Feb 2026), C3 "Exact Is Easier" (Mar 2026), CCPO (Mar 2026), COSAC (Apr 2026), Causal Agent Replay (Jun 2026), REFLECT (Jun 2026) and TraceElephant's "dynamic agentic" attributor all compute credit as *outcome(with role) − outcome(role masked/replaced/patched)*. Because text transcripts are deterministic-enough, exact leave-one-out replay is "simpler, cheaper, and more effective than all approximate alternatives" (C3). This is the mechanism to build.
3. **Optimizers that update prompts of multi-module systems mostly do NOT do credit assignment — they round-robin.** Optimas' scheduler picks a component uniformly at random; GEPA's default `component_selector` is round-robin; DSPy's MIPROv2 searches jointly. The exceptions that *do* attribute before they mutate are CANTANTE (contrastive credit via an attribution LLM, May 2026), the temporal×structural credit paper (rounds × roles, May 2026), GBC (token-level gradient-based connection weights, SIGDIAL 2026) and Optimas' per-component Local Reward Functions (ICLR 2026).
4. **The cheapest reliable signal in a 26-hour window is a trace-judge + replay-confirmation pair**: an LLM scorer over the Weave call tree proposes (role, step), and one bounded counterfactual replay confirms or refutes it (REFLECT's "diagnosis-specific patch → outcome flip"). Feasible with `client.get_calls`, `call.children()`, `call.apply_scorer(...)` and `call.feedback.add(...)`.
5. **Multi-agent RL credit assignment (MAPoRL, Optima, JoyAgents-R1, MARFT, AgentFlow/Flow-GRPO, SHARP, C3) is real but is a multi-day compute job**, not a hackathon mechanism. It is the correct long-term "power unit" story for W&B Serverless RL: once blame says *which role*, train only that role's LoRA with a role-specific reward.
6. **Workflow-structure search (AFlow, MaAS, MAS-ZERO, EvoMAC) attributes at the topology level, not the role level**, and 2026 replication work warns that auto-generated MAS "consistently underperform CoT-SC despite being up to 10x more expensive" (Illusion of Multi-Agent Advantage, Jun 2026) and that self-organizing teams lose up to 41.1% versus their best member (ICML 2026). Keep the F1 team small and role-typed; do not search topologies live.
7. **Harness-evolution evaluation is under attack**: "Rethinking the Evaluation of Harness Evolution" (Jul/Aug 2026) shows evolution "does not consistently outperform simple test-time scaling" and overfits the search benchmark — which is exactly why SCRUTINEER's sealed circuit + ghost car + cost cap is the right framing.
8. **Weave already has the primitives**: `trace_id`/`parent_id` tree, `attributes` for role tags, `apply_scorer` results stored as feedback, and `get_calls(include_feedback=True)` for the blame ledger. No sponsor hack needed.

## Findings with citations

### A. Optimizers that decide *which component* to change

**F1. Optimas — globally aligned local rewards (ICLR 2026).** One Local Reward Function (LRF) per component, sharing an LLM backbone with component-specific linear heads, trained with a pairwise log-sigmoid ranking loss on preference pairs built by running the system up to component *k*, sampling two outputs, and estimating each one's expected *global* reward by downstream Monte Carlo. Component types: prompts (candidates ranked by OPRO, highest local reward wins), trainable weights (PPO with the LRF as critic), discrete configs / model selection (sampling by local reward). **Component selection is uniform random** ("Scheduler: choose a component to optimize" from {1..K}). Five systems: Amazon, PubMedQA, STaRK-Prime, HotpotQA, BigCodeBench; +11.92% average relative over Unoptimized/REINFORCE/LLMSelector/HBC/TextGrad/DSPy, the "only method that improves across all five"; DSPy *hurt* Amazon by 14.3%. Cost: ~0.71k equivalent full-system runs vs 0.80k (TextGrad) and 0.79k (DSPy); 25% of preference data recovers 95.1% alignment; 1B–8B backbones suffice. arXiv 2507.03041 v4 (9 Feb 2026), https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.03041 ; details https://arxiv.org/html/2507.03041v4 ; site https://optimas.stanford.edu. **Hackathon:** the LRF idea (a per-role reward head) is a day-2 build at best; the *preference-pair construction* (two candidate role outputs → downstream MC → which wins) is buildable and is itself a credit signal.

**F2. TextGrad — textual gradients (arXiv Jun 2024; Nature 639:609–616, 2025).** "Backpropagates textual feedback provided by LLMs to improve individual components of a compound AI system"; PyTorch-style API; GPQA 51%→55% zero-shot GPT-4o, +20% relative on LeetCode-Hard. https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07496 (11 Jun 2024); Nature listing https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/497d1aaa0f80b90b72e1a7519f1e4d1aefa2e429 (2025; exact issue date UNVERIFIED, PubMed 40108317 blocked). Credit assignment is *implicit*: the backward LLM writes a critique for every upstream variable, so every component gets a "gradient" — there is no selection step. Used as a baseline in Optimas where it underperforms. **Hackathon:** trivially feasible for one role's prompt; poor at deciding *which* role.

**F3. Microsoft Trace / OPTO / OptoPrime (NeurIPS 2024).** "Optimization with Trace Oracle": the optimizer receives the *execution trace* plus feedback and updates parameters; execution traces "function similarly to gradients." OptoPrime updates prompts, code, hyperparameters, numbers. Library `pip install trace-opt`; API `node(value, trainable=True)`, `@bundle(trainable=True)`, `optimizer = OptoPrime(fn.parameters())`, `optimizer.zero_feedback(); optimizer.backward(result, feedback); optimizer.step()`; optimizers OptoPrime, TextGrad, OPRO; multi-agent demo in VirtualHome; last README news item 2025-02-07. https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.16218 (rev 1 Nov 2024); https://microsoft.github.io/Trace/ ; README https://github.com/microsoft/Trace. Credit assignment: the "Minimal Subgraph" shows the optimizer which trainable params connect to the output; the LLM then decides what to change — attribution is delegated to the optimizer LLM. **Hackathon:** feasible; the *trace-as-gradient* idea is the closest published analogue to "FIA telemetry drives the upgrade."

**F4. GEPA — reflective prompt evolution (ICLR 2026 Oral).** Samples trajectories, reflects in natural language on execution traces + evaluator textual feedback, mutates one module's prompt, keeps a per-instance Pareto frontier, samples candidates "weighting probabilities by how many tasks each candidate leads," and merges lineages by "picking the best version of different modules from each" (System Aware Merge, +≤5%). **Module choice is round-robin** ("selected based on a policy (round-robin)"). Results (Qwen3-8B): vs GRPO@24k rollouts +19.0 HotpotQA, +2.73 IFBench, +13.66 HoVer, +5.19 PUPA, −6.0 AIME-2025, +0.7 LiveBench-Math with 1.8k–7k rollouts ("up to 35x fewer"); +10% aggregate over MIPROv2. https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.19457 (rev 14 Feb 2026); https://arxiv.org/html/2507.19457. Library: `pip install gepa`; `gepa.optimize(seed_candidate={...named components...}, trainset, valset, task_lm, reflection_lm, max_metric_calls≈100–500, adapter, candidate_selection_strategy, use_merge, component_selector)`; adapters implement `evaluate(candidate)` and `make_reflective_dataset(traces)`; https://github.com/gepa-ai/gepa (fetched 2026-09-03). Selector protocol: `__call__(state, trajectories, subsample_scores, candidate_idx, candidate) -> list[str]`; shipped selectors are `RoundRobinReflectionComponentSelector` and `AllReflectionComponentSelector` only — https://github.com/gepa-ai/gepa/blob/main/src/gepa/strategies/component_selector.py. **Hackathon:** very feasible; the *hook* is `component_selector` — plug our blame ledger in so GEPA mutates the blamed role instead of round-robin.

**F5. DSPy optimizers.** Documented set: GEPA (reflection on trajectory → instructions), MIPROv2 (Bayesian optimization over instructions + demos), SIMBA (mini-batch, introspective rules), BootstrapFewShotWithRandomSearch, BootstrapFinetune ("distills a prompt-based DSPy program into weight updates"), BetterTogether (prompt + weight sequences), Ensemble. Guidance: ~10 examples → BootstrapFewShot; 50+ → RandomSearch; 200+ / 40+ trials → MIPROv2. `optimizer.compile(program, trainset=...)`. https://github.com/stanfordnlp/dspy/blob/main/docs/docs/learn/optimization/optimizers.md (fetched 2026-09-03). None of these attribute failure to a module; they search the joint space. **Hackathon:** feasible as the "upgrade applier" for one role.

**F6. CANTANTE — contrastive credit attribution (May 2026).** Each per-agent local optimizer proposes K candidate prompts; K *joint* configurations are assembled (K rollouts per query, not K^N); configurations are partitioned into comparison groups of g=3; an attribution LLM sees the trajectories + system scores and outputs a scalar credit c∈[−1,+1] per agent per configuration; credits average over queries and feed each agent's local optimizer (CAPO default). 3-seed, 10M-token budget: MBPP 41.89% vs MIPROv2 18.42 / GEPA 22.96 (+18.93pp); GSM8K 82.33 vs 69.80 / 61.27; HotpotQA 11.93 vs 14.20 (−2.27pp); best average rank 1.44 vs 2.33. Systems: 3-agent planner/executor/validator, 4-agent 3×executor+aggregator, 4-agent retriever/reader/synthesizer/checker. Code https://github.com/finitearth/cantante. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13295 (13 May 2026); mechanism https://arxiv.org/html/2605.13295. **Hackathon:** feasible in reduced form — 3 joint configurations per race, one attribution-LLM call per group; the "credit correlation analysis" is a good REGS-panel chart.

**F7. Temporal × structural credit for multi-agent prompt optimization (May 2026; ICML 2026 companion "From Interaction Trajectories to Prompt Rules").** Temporal credit = a critic score for each round's aggregation output (C^time_t); structural credit = fusion of role self-score and a peer-view proxy from a single judge over the round's outputs (C^role_i averaged over rounds). **Both computed from the single logged trajectory — no extra rollouts.** Then discrete "verbalized block coordinate descent": Phase A rewrites low-credit *role* prompts with aggregators fixed; Phase B rewrites low-credit *round* aggregators with roles fixed. AQuA/MedMCQA/GPQA/MMLU with Qwen2.5-7B, LLaMA3-8B, Gemma-7B; e.g. MedMCQA/LLaMA3 54.13→61.13 (+7.0), MMLU/LLaMA3 debate 68.78→74.77 (+6.0) vs DSPy MIPRO. No code URL. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30227 (28 May 2026); https://arxiv.org/html/2605.30227 ; ICML poster https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/poster/61502. **Hackathon:** the most directly buildable *judge-only* credit scheme: laps = rounds, roles = agents, one judge call per lap.

**F8. GBC — gradient-based connections (SIGDIAL 2026).** Models the MAS as a computational graph, "introduces gradient-based connection weights to quantify the influence of each agent's output on downstream agents at the token level," builds an attribution graph, back-propagates a task loss to find the error source, then targets prompt edits; MultiWOZ and τ-bench; "higher attribution quality correlat[es] to greater optimization effectiveness." Code https://github.com/yxc-cyber/AgentChord. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28187 (26 Jun 2026). **Hackathon:** needs white-box logits of an open model — feasible only on a local/CoreWeave-hosted model; skip for the demo.

**F9. LLMSelector — per-module model selection (Feb 2025).** Two empirical insights: end-to-end performance is "often monotonic in how well each module performs with all other modules held fixed," and "per-module performance can be estimated accurately by an LLM"; iteratively selects one module and assigns the model with the best module-wise estimate; 5–70% gains across self-refine, multi-agent-debate, locate-solve. https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.14815 (20 Feb 2025); code https://github.com/LLMSELECTOR/LLMSELECTOR. **Hackathon:** feasible; this is exactly "swap the power unit supplier for one role."

### B. Workflow / structure search (attributes to topology, not role)

**F10. AFlow (ICLR 2025 Oral).** MCTS over code-represented workflows; +5.7% avg over SOTA on six benchmarks; smaller models beat GPT-4o "at 4.55% of its inference cost." https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10762 (rev 15 Apr 2025); oral https://iclr.cc/virtual/2025/oral/31731 ; code https://github.com/FoundationAgents/AFlow. Credit: tree-structured experience per node; blame is at whole-workflow granularity.

**F11. MaAS — agentic supernet (ICML 2025 Oral).** Probabilistic distribution over architectures sampled per query; 6–45% of inference cost of handcrafted/automated MAS, +0.54–11.82%. https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.04180 (rev 9 Jun 2025); https://icml.cc/virtual/2025/oral/47201 ; code https://github.com/bingreeky/MaAS.

**F12. MAS-ZERO (May 2025).** Inference-time MAS design with meta-feedback on "solvability and completeness"; up to +16.69 reasoning, +16.66 coding, +5.45 agentic. https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.14996 ; code https://github.com/SalesforceAIResearch/MAS-Zero.

**F13. EvoMAC and textual-backprop MAS.** EvoMAC verifies the coding team's output against a target proxy (tests) and uses "textual backpropagation to update the network" (prompts and team composition); rSDE-Bench. https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.16946 (22 Oct 2024). "Self-Evolving Multi-Agent Systems via Textual Backpropagation": forward decomposition into layered agent teams, backward phase propagates error signals so agents "self-evolve their roles, prompts, and coordination"; seven benchmarks. https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09046 (rev 16 Jun 2026). **Hackathon:** the textual-backprop pattern (test failures → which layer/role) is feasible for the pit-crew/tests stage.

### C. Multi-agent RL for LLM teams (real credit assignment, multi-day compute)

**F14. MAPoRL (ACL 2025).** Verifier scores each turn; an *influence-aware* reward adds "the impact of this solution on the future answers of all agents" over remaining turns with discount γ; shaping params α₀,α₁ (own revision), β₀,β₁ (persuading others); independent PPO policies per agent conditioned on shared history; GSM8K 1,319 / ANLI 1,200 with Phi-3-mini, Qwen2.5-3B, Llama-3-8B; off-the-shelf models do not improve across turns, co-trained ones do. https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.18439 (rev 12 Jul 2025); https://arxiv.org/html/2502.18439.

**F15. Optima (Oct 2024, rev Feb 2025).** Generate→rank→select→train with reward balancing task performance, token efficiency and readability; SFT/DPO/hybrid; MCTS-style DPO data; "up to 2.8x performance gain with less than 10% tokens." https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.08115 ; https://chenweize1998.github.io/optima-project-page.

**F16. JoyAgents-R1.** Master + specialised sub-agents; VR-GRPO Monte-Carlo samples from an initial trajectory to avoid joint-action explosion, then updates only the top-K sampling groups "with maximal reward fluctuations based on the marginal benefit principle"; memory evolution reuses GRPO rewards. https://openreview.net/forum?id=U7n8gZGyAu ; review https://www.themoonlight.io/en/review/joyagents-r1-joint-evolution-dynamics-for-versatile-multi-llm-agents-with-reinforcement-learning (2025). Credit: *which sub-agent gets a gradient step* is chosen by reward variance — a directly reusable rule ("upgrade the role whose lap-time variance is highest").

**F17. MARFT (Apr 2025).** Flex-POMDP formalism and a universal fine-tuning framework for LLM-based MAS. https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.16129v2.

**F18. AgentFlow / Flow-GRPO (ICLR 2026 Oral).** Planner/executor/verifier/generator with evolving memory; *only the planner is trained*; "a single verifiable trajectory outcome broadcasts to every turn"; +14.9 search, +14.0 agentic, +14.5 math, +4.1 science; 7B beats GPT-4o. https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.05592 (rev 22 Jul 2026); https://agentflow.stanford.edu/. Credit: none across modules — it fixes the trained module a priori.

**F19. 2026 counterfactual-credit family.**
- SHARP (Feb 2026, rev Jun 2026): per worker m, `credit_{i,m} = R_acc(τ_i) − R_acc(τ_i \ m)` (agent masked, single pass — a Shapley *approximation*); planner credited by mean positive worker credit; GRPO with per-agent group normalization; rewards weighted α=0.9 broadcast, β=0.9 marginal, γ=0.1 tool-process; +23.66% vs single-agent, +14.05% vs MAS; MuSiQue 50.76, GAIA-text 33.70. https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08335 ; https://arxiv.org/html/2602.08335v2.
- C3 "Exact Is Easier" (Mar 2026, rev May 2026): fix the full interaction history at each decision point, sample alternative actions under the frozen behaviour policy, parameter-free leave-one-out baseline; exact counterfactuals are "simpler, cheaper, and more effective than all approximate alternatives"; six benchmarks, two topologies; checkpoint restoration cuts tokens. https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.06859 ; code https://github.com/EIT-EAST-Lab/C3.
- CCPO (Mar 2026, rev Jun 2026): SCM over trajectories; credit = realized joint outcome − counterfactual with the agent removed; SEPO variant uses verifier-anchored self/peer evaluation; gains "vary across models and datasets." https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21563 ; code https://github.com/bhai114/ccpo.
- COSAC (Apr 2026): critic-free per-agent policy gradient; additive reward decomposition by ridge regression; counterfactual advantages via fictitious continuations; tested with four Qwen3-0.6B agents on ARC. https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.17693.
- GraphGPO (ICML 2026): merges all rollouts into one state-transition graph and credits each edge by reduction in graph distance to goal. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26684 ; https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/poster/60543.
- Survey "From Reasoning to Agentic: Credit Assignment in RL for LLMs" (69 papers to 31 Jul 2026, v. 9 Aug 2026), with a six-diagnostic framework and "CA-ID Card" per claim; repo https://github.com/xxzcc/Awesome-Credit-Assignment-in-LLM-RL. https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.09459.
- "RL for LLM-based MAS through Orchestration Traces" (4 May 2026): eight credit "signal-bearing units from individual tokens to entire teams," eight reward families, a JSON schema for *replayable traces*; notes no paper trains the *stopping* decision. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.02801 ; https://github.com/xxzcc/awesome-llm-mas-rl.

### D. Trajectory-level blame from traces

**F20. Who&When (ICML 2025).** Defines the failure-responsible agent as the one "whose error at a specific time step, if corrected, would change the system outcome from failure to success" and the decisive error step as the earliest such step. 184 tasks / 127 systems (CaptainAgent-generated on GAIA+AssistantBench; hand-crafted Magentic-One), 3 annotators × 23–31 h. Methods with ground truth (algorithm-generated split): all-at-once 54.33% agent / 12.50% step; step-by-step 35.20% / 25.51%; binary search 44.13% / 23.98%; hybrid best step 12.28% at ~149k tokens; ±3–5-step tolerance lifts step accuracy to 30–43%; accuracy collapses beyond ~90 steps; o1/R1 not practically usable; **"statistical-level attribution works better than instance-level."** https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.00212 (rev 2 Jun 2025); https://arxiv.org/html/2505.00212 ; https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/zhang25cq.html ; code https://github.com/mingyin1/Agents_Failure_Attribution. **Hackathon:** all-at-once + step-by-step prompts are a 1-hour Weave Scorer.

**F21. AgenTracer (ICLR 2026 poster, 24 Apr 2026).** TracerTraj-2.5K built by (a) counterfactual replay — an analyzer proposes corrected actions per step, the earliest correction that flips failure→success is the decisive error — and (b) programmatic fault injection into successful runs. AgenTracer-8B (Qwen3-8B) trained with GRPO on format + agent-level binary + step-level Gaussian-decay rewards; 69.62% agent-level on Who&When automated split, ~+18 over Gemini-2.5-Pro, ~+12 over Claude-4-Sonnet; as feedback: MetaGPT/MATH-500 +14.21%, OWL/GAIA +4.8%. https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.03312 (4 Sep 2025); https://arxiv.org/html/2509.03312 ; https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/poster/10007726 ; project https://bingreeky.github.io/atracer/. **Hackathon:** the 8B tracer is a drop-in judge if hosted on CoreWeave (weights link UNVERIFIED); the *fault-injection* recipe is a 2-hour way to make labelled "sabotaged laps" for calibrating our scrutineer.

**F22. TraceElephant (ACL 2026).** 220 failed traces (Captain-Agent 85, Magentic-One 91, SWE-Agent 44) with *full* inputs/outputs/tool logs and a replayable "dynamic" configuration. Static all-at-once 62% agent / 28% step; static agentic (mini-SWE-agent navigating the trace) 66/30; **dynamic agentic (propose candidates, validate by controlled replay) 67/33**; removing inputs drops step accuracy 76% (28→16). https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.22708 (24 Apr 2026); https://arxiv.org/html/2604.22708v1 ; code https://github.com/TraceElephant/TraceElephant. Design consequence: Weave must log *inputs* to every role call, not just outputs.

**F23. The 2026 attribution wave.** AgentLocate (COLM 2026; multi-perspective judges + confidence aggregation + light fine-tuning) https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07989 (8 Jul 2026). StepFinder (KDD 2026; temporal+attention model, no text generation, −79% inference time) https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03467 (2 Jun 2026), code https://github.com/taiyu-zhu/StepFinder. DuoTrace "Detect Before You Attribute" (30 Aug 2026; VAE anomaly detection over dual-view Tree-LSTM trace encodings, +8.7 agent / +7.0 step across six LLM attributors) https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29646. REFLECT (8 Jun 2026; diagnose → "controlled replay with a diagnosis-specific patch" → outcome flips as contrastive evidence; works without ground-truth answers) https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09071. Causal Agent Replay (6 Jun 2026; SCM + intervention algebra + point-of-commitment rule + budget-bounded Monte-Carlo Shapley, effects with CIs; open source https://github.com/jaineet17/causal-agent-replay) https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08275. Knowledge-Based Zero-Replay Debugging (11 Jun 2026; event knowledge graph over routing/memory/tool-use; BranchPoint-Latent predictor lifts Branch Recall@5 0.73→0.93 with zero oracle replays) https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14805. FlowFixer (3 Jul 2026; symbolic traces + executable behavioural specs → attribution +4.8–33.1, RCA +15.3–38.8, 71.3% repair success on Dify/Coze/n8n) https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02882. Implicit Execution Tracing (rev 1 Aug 2026; key-conditioned statistical watermark per agent so the *final text alone* attributes segments to agents) https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17445.

**F24. MAST taxonomy (Mar 2025, rev Oct 2025).** 14 failure modes in 3 categories with frequencies — system design 43.8% (disobey task spec 11.8, disobey role spec 1.5, step repetition 15.7, lost history 2.8, unaware of stopping 12.4); inter-agent misalignment 32.15% (conversation reset 2.2, fail to clarify 6.8, derailment 7.4, withholding 0.85, ignored input 1.9, reasoning-action mismatch 13.2); task verification 24.5% (premature termination 6.2, incomplete verification 8.2, incorrect verification 9.1). o1 few-shot annotator κ=0.77 (0.79 OOD). Interventions: ChatDev CEO-final-authority +9.4%, high-level verification +15.6%. https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.13657 ; https://arxiv.org/html/2503.13657 ; https://github.com/multi-agent-systems-failure-taxonomy/MAST ; `pip install agentdash`. **Hackathon:** MAST modes map one-to-one onto F1 roles (verification → scrutineer; stopping → race engineer; derailment → strategist), giving a defensible classifier vocabulary.

### E. Cautions the judges will know

**F25.** "The Illusion of Multi-Agent Advantage" (11 Jun 2026): auto-generated MAS "consistently underperform CoT-SC despite being up to 10x more expensive." https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13003. "Multi-Agent Teams Hold Experts Back" (ICML 2026): self-organising teams lose up to 41.1% vs best member via "integrative compromise." https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.01011. MAS-PromptBench (22 Jun 2026): prompt optimisation for MAS faces "an exponentially growing search space" with configuration-dependent gains; code https://github.com/juyangbai/MAS-PromptBench, https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23664. "Build, Judge, Optimize" (rev 30 Apr 2026): Sub-agent GEPA "optimizes individual agent nodes against localized rubrics" vs MAMuT GEPA joint multi-turn optimisation with trajectory-level scoring. https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03565. "Rethinking the Evaluation of Harness Evolution for Agents" (rev 27 Aug 2026): on Terminal-Bench 2.1 with GPT-5.4 and Claude Opus 4.6, harness evolution "does not consistently outperform simple test-time scaling methods and exhibits limited generalization"; demands matched-budget baselines and held-out tasks. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12227. "LLM Agents Are Not Always Faithful Self-Evolvers" (ICML 2026): agents "often disregard or misinterpret condensed experience." https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.22436. VeRO (ICML 2026): Scale's harness for agents optimising agents — versioned snapshots, budget-controlled evaluation, structured execution traces; https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22480 ; https://github.com/scaleapi/vero. AEvo "Harnessing Agentic Evolution" (13 May 2026): meta-agent edits the *procedure* rather than the candidate. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13821. NeurIPS 2026 "Managing Agents that Manage Agents" workshop explicitly solicits "credit assignment across modules" for DSPy/GEPA/Trace-style systems. https://meta-agents-workshop.github.io/.

### F. The Weave surface we can build blame on (fetched 2026-09-03)

- Call schema: `id`, `project_id`, `op_name`, `display_name`, `trace_id`, `parent_id`, `started_at`, `ended_at`, `attributes` ("user-defined metadata"), `inputs`, `output`, `exception`, `summary`, `wb_user_id`, `wb_run_id`, `deleted_at`; `trace_id` groups a run, `parent_id` builds the tree. https://docs.wandb.ai/weave/guides/tracking/call-schema-reference

## Design implications for the F1 loop

**The three mechanisms I would actually ship, in order of trust:**

**1. PARC FERMÉ REPLAY — role-masking counterfactual credit (SHARP/C3 pattern) on Weave traces.** Every role is a `@weave.op` whose call carries `attributes={"role": "aero"}` and full `inputs`. After a slow lap, for each role r: fetch the trace with `client.get_calls(filter=CallsFilter(trace_ids=[t]), include_feedback=True)`, find r's call, and re-run the *downstream* pipeline from that call's recorded `inputs` with r's output replaced by (a) the GHOST CAR's version of r (previous accepted generation) and (b) a null/baseline stub. `credit_r = laptime(actual) − laptime(with r replaced)`; report with a small bootstrap CI (Causal Agent Replay reports CIs; Who&When warns single instances are unreliable). Write it back as `call.feedback.add("scrutineer.credit", {"role": r, "delta_s": ..., "ci": [...]})`. Cost: K roles × 1–2 partial replays (downstream only — C3's "fixed history" trick). This is the *only* mechanism in the literature with near-ground-truth status, and it is exactly the definition Who&When and AgenTracer use to label their datasets.

**2. STEWARDS' INQUIRY — trace-judge blame, confirmed by a patch-replay (Who&When hybrid + REFLECT).** A `weave.Scorer` reads the full call tree (inputs *and* outputs — TraceElephant shows dropping inputs costs 76% of step accuracy), runs all-at-once for the agent and step-by-step for the step, classifies the fault with the 14 MAST modes, and emits `{role, step, mode, explanation}`. The scrutineer then applies REFLECT's rule: patch that one step, replay once; the verdict stands only if lap time flips. If AgenTracer-8B weights are obtainable, host it on CoreWeave as the judge; otherwise a frontier model with Who&When's prompts (expect ~55–65% agent-level; the replay check absorbs the errors). Cost: 1 judge call + 1 replay.

**3. CONSTRUCTORS' STANDINGS — statistical blame ledger driving a GEPA `component_selector`.** Credits from (1) and (2) accumulate per role across races in Weave feedback (`get_calls(..., include_feedback=True).to_pandas()`). Upgrade selection uses JoyAgents-R1's marginal-benefit rule (highest reward variance / most negative cumulative credit) instead of GEPA's round-robin, by passing a custom `component_selector(state, trajectories, subsample_scores, candidate_idx, candidate)` that returns only the blamed role's prompt key. GEPA then does the actual upgrade (reflection over that role's trajectories + Pareto frontier over circuits), and DSPy `BootstrapFinetune` / W&B Serverless SFT-RL is the "power unit" path for weight-level upgrades once blame is stable. This answers the temporal×structural paper's block-coordinate-descent structure: Phase A = upgrade a role, Phase B = upgrade the race engineer's aggregation.

**Role mapping for attribution (what a "role" is, so replay can mask it):**

| F1 role | Component masked/replaced in replay | Typical MAST modes it owns | Upgrade lever |
|---|---|---|---|
| Race engineer | outer improver's upgrade-package prompt / stopping policy | 1.5 unaware of stopping, 1.3 step repetition | GEPA on its prompt; JoyAgents-style selection rule |
| Strategist | planning / search-policy module | 2.3 task derailment, 1.1 disobey task spec | prompt + tool budget (LLMSelector-style model swap) |
| Aero | retrieval / context-shaping (what the car "sees") | 1.4 loss of history, 2.5 ignored input | memory/retrieval config (Optimas discrete config) |
| Power unit | base model / LoRA of the solver | raw capability | Serverless SFT/RL LoRA (post-hackathon path) |
| Tyres | sampling temperature / decoding & retry settings | 2.6 reasoning-action mismatch | Optimas hyperparameter sampling |
| Data / telemetry | tool wrappers & parsers | 2.2 fail to clarify, 2.4 withholding | tool schema / code (Trace @bundle trainable) |
| Scrutineer | verifier prompt | 3.1–3.3 verification modes | verifier rubric; MAST +15.6% precedent |

**Rules the evidence forces:**
- Upgrade one role per generation (Optimas, GEPA, LLMSelector all update one component at a time); never on one lap's evidence (Who&When "statistical-level").
- Log `inputs` on every role call (TraceElephant); tag `attributes.role` and `attributes.generation` so replay can reconstruct the exact context (C3 "fixed history").
- Evaluate on the sealed circuit with matched budgets and a ghost-car baseline (Rethinking Harness Evolution: compare with test-time-scaling at equal budget; Illusion paper: compare with a single-agent CoT-SC car).
- Keep the team small and role-typed (Teams Hold Experts Back: losses grow with team size).
