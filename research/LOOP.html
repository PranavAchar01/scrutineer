Scrutineer: The Optimizer

Ten technical roles on an F1 pit wall, each an agent with its own part number. After every race the stewards prove which engineer cost the lap by replaying the race without them. Only that engineer gets upgraded. The upgrade is homologated by a debrief that must reproduce the evidence, and every promotion carries a signed receipt, or a black flag.

A loop that observes, evaluates, tracks, documents and self-improves, and refuses in two ways: when someone cheats, and when the evidence is thin.

The sentence a judge should say afterwards. "It told me which engineer cost the lap, proved it by replaying without them, refused to upgrade anyone when the evidence was thin, upgraded only that engineer when it wasn't, and showed me the one time it caught itself cheating."

The loop

Technically: the team is a directed graph of role nodes, each owning exactly one upgradeable artifact (a skills directory, a harness module, a typed threshold table, a versioned dataset, or a LoRA). One generation is one block-coordinate-descent step that minimises lap time on a sealed circuit the optimizer never sees, under a fixed cost cap. What separates it from "mine traces, patch the prompt" is that credit is computed by counterfactual replay, accumulated statistically before anyone acts, spent on one node chosen by gain per dollar, and promoted only through gates the mutating agent cannot write, with the debrief document itself as one of those gates.

The cast

Five roles on the car (in-lap), four on the pit wall (round-level), one in the garage, plus the human. Kept small on purpose: self-organising teams lose up to 41% against their best member as they grow. Part-number prefixes tell the audience what kind of upgrade landed: P- prompt, R- references and context, S- search policy or thresholds, H- harness code or tool schema, W- weights, C- circuit (curriculum).

Credit assignment: from a trace to "upgrade the aerodynamicist"

Lap time is a utility mapped to seconds: U = 0.5·pass + 0.25·(1 − cost/cap) + 0.25·(1 − wall/300), lap_s = 60 + 40·(1 − U). Pass is execution feedback from hidden tests, never a judge. Every link below is an addressable object.

The selection rule

eligible = { r : n_r ≥ 5 and CI90_lo(blame_r) > 0 }                 # never on one lap
if generalisation_gap > 0.25 or zero_variance_groups ≥ 0.40:        # pit-wall CIRCUIT branch
    r* = SIMULATOR; path = circuit                                   # its own generation; ghost re-timed on vN+1
elif agent_error on ≥ 60% of confirmed incidents and no harness role flips ≥ 60%:
    r* = POWER_UNIT; path = weights                                  # the only route to a weight update
elif override_pick in next_round_config[n-1]:
    r* = override_pick; rule_fired = "principal_override"
elif eligible == ∅:
    r* = None; rule_fired = "no_upgrade"                             # run the matched-budget test-time-scaling ghost
else:
    r* = argmax_{r ∈ eligible}  blame_r · p_fix(r) / (cost_r + λ·hours_r)   # gain per dollar, λ = $0.50/h
    ties → highest var_r
Phase B (every third accepted generation, pit-wall service roles, first eligible wins):
    RACE_ENGINEER  if fix_precision < 0.4 or regression_precision < 0.2 over its last 3 manifests
    ROUTER schema  if router_precision < 0.5 over 3 rounds           # the Scrutineer's own θ
    HISTORIAN      if marimo-check first-pass < 0.6 or page utility < 0.3
    PIT_CREW       if local-pass / sandbox-fail rate > 0.2

p_fix(r) is the fraction of that role's past upgrades that moved OFFICIAL, with a labelled prior of 0.5 until three outcomes exist. Every node has a cost row so gain per dollar is computable for all ten: a harness patch is about $0.30 and 15 minutes, a threshold refit about $0.03 and 2 minutes, an RL job about $0.50 to $1.50 and 2 hours. The improver is scored on its own forecast next round, and the router has meta-credit: if its precision falls below 0.5 over three rounds, the router schema becomes the next target. That is the "colony keeping each other honest" archetype made literal.

Worked example

  1. Lap 14, generation 7: hidden tests fail. The router says AERO 0.61, POWER UNIT 0.27, confidence 0.58, ablation needed.
  2. AERO's artifact is unchanged since generation 6, so patch-replay: the BlameScorer inserts the missing reference into AERO's output; the downstream re-run passes twice. Credit to AERO: +12.8 s. The null-stub replay fails, so AERO is load-bearing, just wrong.
  3. Nine such incidents this generation: blame +38 s, CI90 [21, 55], n = 9. Eligible. STRATEGIST has n = 4 and is not, despite a higher gain per dollar.
  4. selection:gen-7 = AERO by gain per dollar. The Historian's pattern page 0042 links nine call refs, and each call links back to the page.
  5. The Race Engineer reads the selection object and the page, emits AERO R-v4 diffs A and B plus a manifest. The pit crew installs; A wins quali.
  6. Sealed: quali +0.9 s, sealed +0.7 s, smoke +0.4 s, all the same sign; no per-item regression; cost $2.41 of $3.00; verdict LEGAL 0.93; the debrief reproduces all of it, gate_ok true.
  7. APPROVE. Registry aero:v4 gets champion, the chain row is signed, and the upgrade-scene caption is generated from selection → pattern → diff hash → version. It cannot be written without that chain.

Real vs simulated, and the 26-hour plan

Task: Python function synthesis with hidden tests, 120 items split 30 quali, 30 sealed, 60 reserve for later circuits. Execution-verifiable, so RL reward is deterministic. Three people: A builds the loop control plane, B the broadcast, C the sponsor rails. Hour zero is Saturday 11:15.

Real, end to end

Weave tracing of every role with inputs; quali and sealed evaluations in two teams; the feedback ledger, standings and selection objects; per-role W&B runs, Registry links and the leaderboard. Typed router with three-mode replay and fault-injection calibration. Strategist typed calls with RETIRE and a real threshold refit. Race Engineer as headless Claude Code with read-only MCP producing diffs and a manifest; pit crew sandbox install; Scrutineer typed verdict; all gates; signed chain; manifest check. Historian's wiki, debrief notebook, W&B Report and bidirectional links. One Serverless RL job on the 14B base started at hour 4, rollouts eight-way concurrent. Simulator mining a real second circuit. Unattended mode overnight. ARIA used live on the per-role runs.

Simulated, labelled as such

3D car physics and animation (presentation only, driven by real versions and real deltas). Fallback confidence from a three-sample vote, labelled UNCALIBRATED. One seeded tampering diff if no natural black flag appears by Sunday 10:00, labelled "seeded" on screen and in the Report. The p_fix prior of 0.5 labelled "prior" until three outcomes exist. A molab GPU cell only if it hosts a real workload (a vLLM structured-output backend for the router during calibration).

Prize by prize

Best Loop Design

All five verbs plus audit and promote have a named mechanism and artifact. Credit is proposed by a typed router, verified by replay in three modes, accumulated with CIs, and spent on one node by gain per dollar. The loop refuses in two ways, the improver is scored on its forecasts and can itself be upgraded, and the debrief is a gate, not a tail. None of the prior WeaveHacks trace-mining projects had per-role attribution, an auditor that can reject, a public/private split, or an upgrade scene.

Most Production-Ready

Evaluator, sealed data, lineage and scrutineer live outside the improver's write set by permissions (separate team, service-account key, OS users), the exact failure DGM demonstrated. Every promotion is a signed, hash-chained record with parent links that a 40-line verifier replays. Hundreds of typed decisions per race at cents, an in-lap budget, a declared unattended mode logged in every chain row, a 30-second overseer.

Best Use of Weave

Both data planes for what each does: classic ops with attributes for feedback, scorers and datasets; the conversation SDK for the Agents view with one agent per role and the part number as agent version. Signals as the first hint, Evaluation with trials, EvaluationLogger linked to spans, the ledger as feedback rows, published standings and selection objects, a single-key alias, a Leaderboard, and the improver's own Claude Code session traced by the same telemetry it improves against.

Best Use of ARIA

ARIA reads one W&B run per role per round, the same numbers the ledger used, builds the training board, drafts the season Report, and its next-round hypothesis with the Report URL as provenance is a required input to the Race Engineer; its pick is compared with the ledger's and logged. ARIA can run experiments through a Launch queue; here it is recommend-only by our scoping choice, and the human APPROVE is our gate. REGS says exactly that.

Best Use of marimo

The debrief is executable and causal: app.run(defs) is the hand-off contract to the next round, the upgrade-pick dropdown recomputes next_round_config live, check plus a script-mode run are the homologation gate, and promotion cannot happen without gate_ok. The archive is served per round with a molab link. No published marimo notebook over Weave traces was found during the research; if one exists, this is the first that is a promotion gate.

Best Use of TypeSafe AI

The pit wall is a typed-decision endpoint used where chat models are the wrong tool: unattended, high-frequency, schema-bound decisions whose confidence is measured, not assumed. The strategist, the credit router (low confidence routes to a real replay) and the scrutineer (low confidence refers to the stewards). The stage claim is never "System1 is calibrated" but "here is how calibrated the pit wall was", with the backend named beside the reliability curve and the terms of service respected: decisions, never distillation targets.

How this was made

CompetitionThree independent architectures: the optimizer over a team graph, the living notebook with documentation as the engine, the training camp with roles as policies. Three judges (a hackathon judge, an RSI researcher, a staff engineer who has used the sponsor tools) scored loop rigor, sponsor depth, intuitiveness, novelty and 26-hour feasibility. The optimizer won; the debrief-as-contract, provenance chain and wiki asymmetry were grafted from the notebook design; the typed strategist, simulator, RL timing and gain-per-dollar selection from the training camp. VerificationA completeness critic raised 16 issues (all high and medium fixed, including unattended mode and the sealed-isolation aggregate object). The eight most load-bearing sponsor claims were each attacked by two refuters against live docs and source; five were corrected: ART's real call shape and metrics logging, the SDK path for Signals, marimo's check and app.run edges, ARIA's launch capability (recommend-only here is a scoping choice, not a product limit), and the Sandboxes network options and pricing.
